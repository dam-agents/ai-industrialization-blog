A UX/UI designer's account of working with a personalized agent wired into the team's strategy knowledge base, the product codebase, and a `claude.md` describing who they are, how they work, and what they own. Flow boards for design tickets — flows as rows, assembled from the product's real components so they cannot drift from the shipped UI. First drafts of those boards produced overnight against whatever tickets are open, so the morning starts with something to react to instead of a blank canvas. Coded prototypes on their own branch that developers pull as the reference for interaction design. Meeting prep compiled the morning of rather than going stale, and a running list of what the designer still needs from each meeting or has waiting on feedback. Plus codebase audits for usability problems and places the interface could be clearer. The argument: a design agent earns its keep by reading the same source of truth the product ships from — pointing it at a static style guide would have produced none of this.
