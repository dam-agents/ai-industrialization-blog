Most team inefficiency is not people working slowly. It is people working on the
wrong thing, working from missing or wrong context, or working inside a process
that quietly wastes their time. All three are visible in the artefacts a team
already produces — issues, epics, plans, the trail of how work actually moved —
and all three are exactly the kind of pattern an agent will find because it will
read every one of them and a person will not.

The worked example is the team's own work on issues, epics and planning:
what tightening those up actually changed, and what the agent noticed that
nobody had written down. The wider argument is that operational discipline
normally decays because it depends on someone having the attention to enforce it
on a busy week. An agent doesn't have busy weeks. It can keep checking that an
epic still has a stated outcome, that an issue carries the context needed to
start it, that the plan and the board have not silently diverged — and keep
steering the team back rather than filing a report nobody reads.

Worth taking positions on: which signals are real and which are process theatre;
that the point is to surface the problem to the team, not to score people; and
where this stops — an agent can say the context is missing, but it cannot decide
what the team should care about this quarter.
