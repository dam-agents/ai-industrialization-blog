A deep dive with the SMA team, who went from a team of forty to a team of four people who had never seen the codebase — and who say DAM was instrumental in making that handover work. The interesting part is not the headcount, it is that the knowledge which normally lives in forty people's heads had to come from somewhere else, and coding agents with access to the code were where it came from.

What the post needs from the team: what the four actually asked the agents in their first weeks, which questions the codebase could answer and which ones still needed a human who had left, and what they would tell the next team inheriting something this size. Worth getting specific about the failure modes too — where the agents were confidently wrong about intent, and how the four learned to tell the difference.

This is the first proposed topic about a team that is not ours using DAM, which is most of its value as a post: external evidence rather than our own account of our own tooling.
