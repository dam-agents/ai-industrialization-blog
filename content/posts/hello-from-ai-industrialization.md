Artificial intelligence is remaking what a research organization can do. Most teams start by pointing it at their product. We started by pointing it at the research itself — at how experiments get designed, run, and understood inside IBM Research.

This is the first post on a blog we intend to keep. Before anything else, an introduction: who we are, where we started, and what we plan to write about here.

## 01 — Who we are

AI Industrialization operates across the division to bring AI-native research to the core of its biggest bets, building common ground in agents, experimentation, inference, and data. We are not a pillar of our own — we embed with the teams that are, and what we learn with one of them becomes infrastructure the next can pick up.

Computing is where this can move fastest, from the physics of quantum devices to the chips and compilers that power the next era of computation. That is where we started.

> Experiments should be bounded by ideas, not by capacity.

## 02 — Where we started

The work begins with a handful of priority workloads — quantum calibration, error correction and compilation; chip design; compiler design and kernel synthesis. Each one shortens the path from idea to result, and each one strengthens the platform for the next.

Researchers author design–build–test–learn loops as ordinary code, and the platform observes them live: every stage, span, and score. That turns agentic exploration into science you can compare across weeks.

It also produces a second asset almost as a byproduct: agent turns, experiment spans, and inference telemetry, collected into an AI-native record of how agents actually solve research problems. Those records are what make the next workload faster than the last.

## 03 — What we'll publish here

Field notes over announcements. What worked on a real workload and what didn't; the decisions we'd defend and the ones we've since reversed; guides concrete enough for another team to follow. We'd rather work in public than present a finished thing.

If something here is useful to you, or wrong, we want to hear it. That's most of the point.
